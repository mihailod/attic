Arian Web Shop iOS App Prototype by Mihailo Despotovic, January 2014

A web shop iPhone app prototype for Arian GmbH, a printing company in Gleisdorf, Austria, from the time of my startup, MiRteh. It was meant to lead to a big project for Arian, which was later cancelled; my main contact there was Rok Planinšec. The prototype demoed product discovery, browsing and searching, a configurator that depends on the product, a shopping cart and account management. It is written in Objective-C for iOS 7, with a storyboard, from December 2013 to February 2014.

Try it

The app, rebuilt for the browser as it looked on an iPhone 5 with iOS 7: its screens as the storyboard lays them out, its pictures and texts, and its logic from the Objective-C. Browse the products, or search them; the Panoramic Canvas, under Marketing Ads, is the one with a configurator, of format, quantity and delivery. To shop, log in first under Account, with any name and password: the login was only pretended, as was the order. In the cart, tap an item to change it, or swipe it to the left to delete it.

The bugs

  • Prices were a cent, or a few euros, off. They were computed in 32-bit floats, which carry about 7 digits, so of the 396 prices a panoramic canvas can have, 100 were shown wrong: 1,000 of the smallest, at 4% off with standard delivery, cost €76.805,00, and the app said €76.804,99. The screenshot below shows it in the real app. Fixed computes in exact cents.
  • Every product was priced as a panoramic canvas, the smallest one if it had no formats: 250 stickers cost €20.005,00. That was a prototype's shortcut, as are the same two pictures and the same text for every other product.
  • Add to Cart first asked for file:///Users/mihailod/Documents/links.html, a file on my Mac, the stand-in for the web shop's server; on a phone that fails, and the item went into the cart anyway.
  • On today's iOS the app crashes twice. At launch, because the first screen calls init a second time on a table cell it got from the storyboard, which iOS 7 let pass; and while searching, because it swapped in the new results and only told the table to reload later, so the table asked for a row that no longer existed.

The real app, today

The original project, built with today's Xcode and run in the iOS simulator, on an iPhone SE, the nearest to the iPhone 5's screen. It needed only a newer minimum iOS version, and three small patches for the two crashes. Today's iOS draws it with its own navigation and tab bars, and, as the app was laid out for the iPhone 5 alone, puts some things where they don't belong: the cart's list doesn't show, and the storyboard's stray “Label” shows through the title. On the phone its name was Coolprint.
