Davor's Trader by Mihailo Despotovic, December 2013

A contract job from the time of my startup, MiRteh, for Davor Urošević, a FOREX trader. His idea was a breakout: watch EUR/USD from 11:00 to 13:30 and note its high and its low, then, from 13:31 to 17:00, buy if the price breaks out above the high, sell if it breaks down below the low, and get out when it turns. It started, in November, as a Java program to sit between his data and MetaTrader, and ended, in December, as an Expert Advisor for MetaTrader 4 itself, written in MQL4, which I kept pasted under the Java code.

The Expert Advisor, in a MetaTrader 4 tester

The MQL4, ported line by line and run in a small MetaTrader 4 strategy tester, tick by tick, as MT4's tester runs it. The market is made up: EUR/USD ticks every few seconds, calmer at night, and on some afternoons with a trend, from Monday, December 2, 2013. Or load real history exported from MetaTrader: 1-minute bars as date,time,open,high,low,close,volume. The made-up market says nothing of how the strategy would do on a real one.

The bugs, and the fixes

    
    
        
  • It sold on no breakout at all. The buy waits for the Ask to rise 4 pips above the high, but the sell's test is
            Bid - lo < pipsEntry: the price anywhere below 4 pips above the low, so a sell could open as soon as
            trading began, with the price sitting just above the low. Fixed, it waits for the price to fall 4 pips below it.
    • 
        
  • A sell's stop loss was 4 pips away. The buy's is negativeStopSell, 30 pips; the sell's used
            positiveStopSell, 4.
    • 
        
  • A position still open at 17:00 was never closed, and the EA never traded again. Positions are only looked after in
            the trading hours, and the new day, which moves the hours on, waits until no position is open.
    • 
        
  • After a weekend, it never traded again either. Each new day moves the hours on by one day, so on the Monday after a
            Friday they point to Saturday.
    • 
        
  • Smaller ones: while sampling, the first price counts only as a high; the log shows the old high and low instead of the new
            ones; pipsProfit isn't used; and a market order gets an expiration time, which is meant for pending orders.
            In the Java program, Process Data looks at the blue program area while its message speaks of the white one.
    • 
    

    
There is no MetaTrader here to check the port against, so it was checked with eight made-up days that test its rules,
        each run as written and fixed; breaking the port on purpose in eight ways, each was caught. As written, the EA traded once
        or twice in the first days of a test and then stopped for good: in 12 made-up markets of two weeks, it traded on 1 to 4 days,
        fixed on 6 to 10.
